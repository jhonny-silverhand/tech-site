# tech//site 3.0 — Editorial Design Research

**Status: research only.** Nothing in this document has been implemented. It's the deliverable for the "3.0" research brief — a synthesis of real references and reusable principles, meant to inform an actual design pass later, not a preview of one.

A note on method before the content: the brief asks for 20–30 Dribbble shots and 10–15 Behance projects per niche. I didn't fabricate a list of specific shot URLs or project names — I have no way to verify specific Dribbble/Behance links are real and still live, and inventing plausible-sounding ones would be worse than not having them at all. What follows instead is built from real, verifiable sources: the named real products from the brief (Linear, Apple Music, Stripe, Perplexity, Anthropic, Nothing, and others), researched properly rather than recalled from memory. The Dribbble/Behance tag pages from the brief are genuinely good next steps for visual curation — that's a browsing session, not a research report, and worth doing together interactively when it's time.

---

## 1. What never changes (recap, not new)

The brief's list — logo, typography, navigation, search, core components, animation language, spacing, interaction patterns — is mostly already true of the current build, which matters: this research is about what can flex *within* that, not a case for loosening it.

- **Logo stays typographic**, exactly as built: `tech//site` in Fraunces, the double-slash as a drawn mark rather than the font's own glyph. Nothing below proposes touching this.
- **Search is already unified**: the Cmd+K / `/` command palette works identically regardless of niche. Any per-niche visual mood should decorate this, never fork it into niche-specific search experiences.
- **Motion is already restrained on principle**, not by accident — the Knowledge Orbit tilts a few degrees toward the cursor rather than auto-rotating, specifically because continuous motion is what `prefers-reduced-motion` users are opting out of. This same restraint should extend to whatever comes out of this research: ambient ≠ animated-for-its-own-sake.
- **Color identity already exists** as a system (`lib/niches.ts`), not a one-off — that's the mechanism any "editorial universe" work below would extend, not replace.

## 2. Cross-niche editorial principles (reusable regardless of niche)

Pulled from the real research, not the brief's own list restated:

**Restraint is a competitive aesthetic, not a lack of ambition.** The clearest pattern across Linear, Stripe, and Anthropic's own site is that a *single* disciplined accent color and one consistent type family read as more premium than visual variety. One analysis of Linear's marketing site put it plainly: a single accent color, one display family, and a four-step surface hierarchy are what make it read as "software-craft documentation" rather than a typical SaaS page. Anthropic's site works the same way in a much noisier category — restraint itself is the differentiator against competitors leaning into speculative visuals. This directly validates the "what never changes" list: the discipline *is* the brand.

**Money should look different from everything else, on purpose.** Stripe's design is well-documented as using monospace numerals specifically for financial figures — not decoration, but so columns align and amounts read as precise rather than approximate. Generous whitespace around financial data functions as a trust signal in the same research: cramped interfaces read as if they're hiding something. tech//site already uses monospace for metadata chrome sitewide; Finance is the one niche where extending that to *body figures* (prices, percentages, comparisons) would be a meaningful, well-justified departure from the shared base.

**Ambient color should come from something real, not be pasted on.** Apple Music's iOS 26 update is built around exactly this: the interface color now derives from the actual playing artwork rather than a fixed theme, turning what one write-up called "a pause between interactions" into something that feels alive without any extra chrome. tech//site already does a version of this (the per-article niche-color wash, shipped in `1.2`) — the research validates the direction rather than suggesting a new one. The one gap: Apple's version reads as *alive* partly because it's tied to something the user chose (their music); ours is tied to an editorial category the user is browsing, which is a slightly different, more passive relationship — worth keeping in mind rather than assuming the two will feel identical.

**Editorial grids are asymmetric on purpose, and that's a typography problem more than a layout one.** The clearest single idea from the print-editorial research: "equal columns are for spreadsheets, not stories" — size contrast (a 64px headline against 16px body) does the hierarchy work that color does elsewhere. This is the actual justification for the Bento grid already shipped, and the strongest argument for extending varied type scale (not just varied tile size) into the Featured section specifically.

**Different AI products already prove "same category, different atmosphere" works, which is the whole premise of this brief.** Claude, ChatGPT, and Perplexity are all "an AI you talk to," and yet one comparative write-up describes them as three distinct reading atmospheres: Claude reads as "warm sepia tones and serif fonts... an almost bookish atmosphere," ChatGPT as "clean, minimalist... black and white... designed for shortened attention spans," and Perplexity as "grey background and hover quotes... an academic atmosphere that builds confidence through transparency." That's the AI Tools niche's actual reference set — not "make it look futuristic," but "reading mode vs. scanning mode vs. citation-forward" are three legitimately different, provable moods within one category.

## 3. Editorial universes — niche by niche

Each entry: the mood, what's real and worth learning from, and — since the brief explicitly wants extraction, not copying — a **one-line "what this actually means here"** translation, kept inside what section 1 says never changes.

### AI Tools — *reading mode, not command-center mode*
**References:** Anthropic (restraint as differentiation in a noisy category), Perplexity (citation-forward, "grey background and hover quotes... academic atmosphere"), Claude's own product ("warm sepia tones and serif fonts... bookish").
**What this means here:** the niche already carries the site's own accent blue (`#4F7DFF`, shared with the global UI on purpose per `2.0`), which is a good fit — AI Tools content is arguably the site's most "flagship" section. The reusable idea worth taking: treat citations/sources in AI Tools articles the way Perplexity treats them (a distinct, slightly set-apart visual treatment for "here's where this claim comes from"), rather than styling them like a normal link.

### Programming — *documentation, not marketing*
**References:** Linear ("opinionated," single-accent, precision typography, motion used only to demonstrate speed, never decoration), GitHub/Vercel (developer-first chrome, monospace-heavy).
**What this means here:** this niche already gets JetBrains Mono weighting naturally (code blocks). The extractable idea: Linear's rule that *motion should demonstrate the product's defining claim, not just look impressive* — for a Programming article specifically, that could mean code block interactions (copy feedback, diff highlighting) get slightly more motion budget than the rest of the site, since here motion is illustrating precision, not decorating prose.

### Android — *confident restraint, not maximalism*
**References:** Nothing OS — genuinely the best-matched real reference in the whole brief. Its philosophy is explicitly "a rebellion against... bloated" design: dot-matrix typography used sparingly, monochrome-plus-one-signal-color, and (as Nothing's own design lead put it) an interface built to feel "alive, playful, and deeply personal" without being loud.
**What this means here:** Android's lime-green accent is already close to Nothing's real-world "signal color on monochrome" approach. The extractable idea isn't "add dot-matrix type" (that's Nothing's specific mark, not ours to borrow) — it's the *ratio*: mostly restrained/monochrome, one small area of real personality per page, never personality spread evenly across everything.

### Windows & Linux — *utility, not atmosphere*
**References:** less of a single clean reference here than the other niches — this content (error fixes, driver updates, terminal commands) is closer to technical documentation than editorial writing.
**What this means here:** honestly, the research suggests this niche should get *less* atmospheric treatment than most, not more — the "Editorial Design Guide" research is explicit that grids like this exist for content people scan and leave, and that's most of what this niche actually is. Worth resisting the urge to force a mood here; clarity and speed of scanning is the correct mood.

### Tech Buying Guides — *(the brief's "Apple," broadened)*
**References:** the brief's own "luxury, minimal, editorial" direction maps to Apple's product pages and Apple Newsroom, but this niche is broader than Apple products specifically — it's every buying decision.
**What this means here:** the useful extraction isn't "make it silver and minimal" (that's Apple's specific palette, and this niche already has its own red per `2.0`) — it's Apple's *pacing*: one product, full attention, before moving to the next, rather than a dense comparison grid. For a "best phones under ₹25,000"-style article, that could mean one large hero treatment per recommended product inside the article rather than a cramped spec table.

### Gaming — *cinematic through scale, not through gore*
**References:** brief cites PlayStation/Xbox/Steam/IGN — all real, all lean on large hero imagery and confident type at large sizes.
**What this means here:** the extractable idea is scale and contrast (big cover art, bold type), which is already partly present via the horizontal rail's album-style covers — the gap is that gaming content specifically would benefit from *larger* hero treatment on the article page itself, more than most other niches need.

### Career & Jobs — *calm and structured, not motivational-poster*
**References:** no strong named reference in the brief for this one (it doesn't map to any of the listed real products) — closest real-world parallel is more editorial career journalism (the tone of a good long-form careers article) than a product.
**What this means here:** structure over inspiration — this content (interview prep, exam guides) is fundamentally a checklist/plan the reader will revisit, so a clear, calm, step-numbered visual treatment matters more than any "mood."

### Finance — *Stripe's trust signals, specifically*
**References:** Stripe, in real depth (see section 2) — monospace for money, generous whitespace as a trust signal, sparing color use.
**What this means here:** this is the strongest, most directly transferable match in the whole research set. Concretely: any specific numbers in a Finance article (percentages, dollar amounts, comparisons) set in the mono face at a *slightly larger* size than surrounding prose, the way Stripe treats figures — small, cheap, and exactly on-brief.

### Productivity — *quiet tool, not showcase*
**References:** brief doesn't name a strong direct product; Notion is mentioned elsewhere in the project as a content topic, and its own real design ethos (calm, quiet, get-out-of-the-way chrome) is a reasonable fit.
**What this means here:** least visual noise of any niche, on purpose — Productivity content is inherently about reducing friction, so a Productivity article looking the *calmest* of all nine is the article practicing what it's writing about.

## 4. What's worth prioritizing first (if and when this becomes an implementation phase)

In rough order, based on where the research most strongly justifies a change over what already exists:

1. **Finance mono-figure treatment** — smallest change, clearest research backing, most "on brand" already.
2. **AI Tools citation styling** — small, contained, and this niche gets the most traffic-shaping attention already (it's the site's de facto flagship section).
3. **Gaming's larger article hero** — a scoped, single-template change.
4. **Windows & Linux and Career & Jobs *not* getting heavier treatment** — this is a decision to make now, in writing, so a future implementation pass doesn't quietly over-decorate the two niches the research says shouldn't be decorated.
5. Everything else in section 3 is directionally right but lower-confidence — worth a real visual pass (mockups, not just principles) before touching code.
