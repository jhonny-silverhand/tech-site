# Cashback vs. Points: Which Credit Cards Actually Pay?

Indian credit cards promise "10X rewards" and "5% cashback" in the same breath. The math behind those claims decides whether you earn ₹500 or ₹5,000 a year. Let's do the math.

## The only number that matters: reward rate

Reward rate = (value you actually redeem ÷ money you spent) × 100. A card advertising "10X points" where 1 point = ₹0.10 gives a **2.5% reward rate** on a ₹100 spend (10 pts × ₹0.10 × 10... check the fine print — "10X" is usually on a base of 1 point per ₹100, i.e., 10 points = ₹1 on ₹100 = 1%).

Always convert to rupees-per-hundred-spent. Marketing multiples are designed to confuse.

## When cashback wins

- **You spend under ₹30K/month.** Points cards earn their keep through milestone bonuses and lounge access that need high spends to unlock.
- **You hate tracking.** Cashback auto-credits or offsets your statement. Points demand Redemption Tuesdays and transfer-partner math.
- **Your spending is unbonused** (rent via third parties, fuel, utilities): a flat 1.5–2% cashback card beats a points card earning base rates.

Example: ₹20,000/month on a 2% flat cashback card = **₹4,800/year**, zero effort.

## When points win

- **You travel.** Airline/hotel transfer partners can push point value to ₹0.50–₹1.00+ each — turning a "4% card" into an 8%+ card on flights you'd buy anyway.
- **You hit milestones.** Many premium cards add ₹10K+ in vouchers at quarterly thresholds. If your organic spend crosses them, effective rates jump.
- **You redeem strategically.** Product-catalog redemptions are a trap (often ₹0.20/point). Transfers and statement credit for travel are where value lives.

## The two-card setup for most people

1. **Flat cashback card** (1.5–2%) for everything unbonused.
2. **Category card** (5%+ on your top category: online shopping, dining, or travel).

This beats any single "super card" for spends under ₹60K/month. Above that, add a premium travel card and run the milestone math yearly.

| Profile | Setup | Expected value |
|---|---|---|
| Student, ₹10K/mo | One no-fee cashback card | ₹1,500–2,400/yr |
| Professional, ₹40K/mo | Flat + category combo | ₹12,000–20,000/yr |
| Frequent flyer, ₹1L/mo | Premium travel + transfers | ₹40,000+/yr |

> Not financial advice. Never carry a balance — 42% APR wipes out every reward ever printed.

## Key takeaways

- Convert everything to reward rate (₹ per ₹100 spent).
- Cashback for simplicity; points for travelers who redeem smart.
- Two cards (flat + category) beat one "super card" for most.
- Never pay interest to earn rewards.
